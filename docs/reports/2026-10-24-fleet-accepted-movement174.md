## Bounded findings

The upstream batch extends its earlier first-destination controls through original fleet movement and real native command dispatch. It distinguishes original fleet-phase instructions, conditional native requests, and full native AI turns; these have different phase histories and are not compared as equivalent whole turns. The original observer/plain runs agree at the complete saved-state boundary, and captured fleet-phase exits replay through unchanged original instructions according to the upstream receipts. Research subsequently replayed the retained original instruction controls and native probes offline; the Wine runs themselves remain upstream observations, not fresh games.

A paired original low-level control changes only the fleet's controller. When a fleet has fewer moves than the next rough-sea step costs, original AI control clears its remaining moves; original human control retains them. The corresponding native `MoveFleetCommand` accepts and leaves the fleet in place with its moves retained under both controllers. The affordable positive control agrees. In the remake source at `933a5d4a`, `MoveFleetCommandHandler` writes `walk.MovesRemaining` directly, while `MoveArmyCommandHandler` applies `MovementAbortRule.MovesAfterAbortedStep` for an insufficient step. This independently supports the native half of the reported discrepancy. Fresh replay reproduces original AI budget 0 versus human budget 1, including `0044E073`, and the retained native build's accepted stationary result with budget 1 for both. This is a historical counterexample at the tested build: [#1408](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/1408) is now **closed, status:merged** (checked 2026-10-24). The replay does not test the post-fix build.

The upstream native AI traces show that some peace, trade and alliance fleet approaches were **accepted commands**, extending the earlier proposal-only observation. Those native weights are explicitly designed. This establishes an executed policy difference on the declared controls, not an unintended bug or a decision to change the designed mode. [#1409](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/1409) scopes faithful selection and destination execution separately from [#1405](https://github.com/diegoami/imperial_conquest_2/issues/1405)'s missing stored-route state.

Synthetic follow-ups exercise stored route with zero moves, cargo carried at sea, and protected versus unprotected adjacent ports. They narrow the earlier report's explicit untested combination and distinguish approach from attack. They do not establish route persistence through native save/load/export, disembarkation or capacity, naval combat outcomes, global RNG equivalence, or native-Windows original acceptance. The recorded original waypoint and port movement cannot be modelled by merely giving the same final coordinate to one native straight walk.
